Locked Pair · Step-by-step example

Two cells in the intersection of a row and box, or column and box, each contain only the same two candidates. Remove both digits from all other cells in both shared regions.

Recognize the pattern

Two cells in the intersection of a row and box, or column and box, each contain only the same two candidates.

How to find it

Find a naked pair, then check whether both cells also share a box as well as their row or column.

What it allows

Remove both digits from all other cells in both shared regions.

Avoid the mix-up

A pair sharing only one region is a naked pair. Both cells must have exactly the two candidates; an extra candidate breaks the lock.

Step-by-step example · Row

This example starts from a saved intermediate position. Its candidates include earlier logical eliminations; they are not fresh automatic notes.

Blue: evidence. Green: conclusion. Crossed-out digits: eliminated candidates. R1C1 means row 1, column 1.

Step 1 · Find the pair

The two highlighted cells contain only 1 and 7. They form a pair: one must be 1, the other 7.

Step 2 · See the shared regions

The pair lies in both row 1 and box 1. These two cells reserve 1 and 7, so no other cell in either region can use them.

Step 3 · Remove the targets

Remove the marked 1, 7 candidates from the other cells in row 1 and box 1.

What it allows

Remove the marked 1, 7 candidates from the other cells in row 1 and box 1.
