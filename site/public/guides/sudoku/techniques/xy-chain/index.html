XY-Chain · Step-by-step example

Each chain cell has exactly two candidates. Neighboring cells see each other and share the link digit; the two ends share the target digit. Remove the shared endpoint digit from a cell that sees both ends.

Recognize the pattern

Each chain cell has exactly two candidates. Neighboring cells see each other and share the link digit; the two ends share the target digit.

How to find it

Trace connected bivalue cells, switching to the other candidate in each cell, until the end carries the starting target digit.

What it allows

Remove the shared endpoint digit from a cell that sees both ends.

Avoid the mix-up

A two-candidate cell with an extra pencil mark is invalid; every link and both endpoint sight lines must be checked.

Step-by-step example · Short chain

This example starts from a saved intermediate position. Its candidates include earlier logical eliminations; they are not fresh automatic notes.

Blue: evidence. Green: conclusion. Crossed-out digits: eliminated candidates. R1C1 means row 1, column 1.

Step 1 · Establish the bivalue chain

Every chain cell is bivalue: R5C8={4, 7}, R5C4={3, 7}, R6C6={2, 3}, R6C5={2, 4}. Inside each cell, the two candidates have a strong relationship—if one is false, the other is forced true. Between different cells, linked candidates with the same digit are mutually exclusive. The two endpoints R5C8=4, R6C5=4 share candidate 4.

Step 2 · Reasoning

Branch 1: suppose R5C8=4 is false.

Step 3 · Reasoning

R5C8=4 is false. R5C8 now has only R5C8=7 left, so it must be true.

Step 4 · Reasoning

Case 1: R5C8 is the bivalue cell R5C8=4, R5C8=7. With its other candidate false, R5C8=7 is forced true under the current assumption. Across cells, it is mutually exclusive with R5C4=7, so cross out those conflicts.

Step 5 · Reasoning

R5C4=7 is false. R5C4 now has only R5C4=3 left, so it must be true.

Step 6 · Reasoning

R5C4 is the next bivalue cell R5C4=3, R5C4=7. With its other candidate false, R5C4=3 is forced true under the current assumption. Across cells, it is mutually exclusive with R6C6=3, so cross out those conflicts.

Step 7 · Reasoning

R6C6=3 is false. R6C6 now has only R6C6=2 left, so it must be true.

Step 8 · Reasoning

R6C6 is the next bivalue cell R6C6=2, R6C6=3. With its other candidate false, R6C6=2 is forced true under the current assumption. Across cells, it is mutually exclusive with R6C5=2, so cross out those conflicts.

Step 9 · Reasoning

At the endpoint bivalue cell R6C5 (R6C5=2, R6C5=4), R6C5=2 is false, so R6C5=4 is forced true under the current assumption. This endpoint excludes the targets R6C8=4, R6C9=4.

Step 10 · Withdraw the previous rectangle filling

Withdraw this assumption and its consequences. Return to the unchanged candidate snapshot before examining the next possibility.

Step 11 · Reasoning

Assume the first endpoint R5C8=4 is true in its bivalue cell R5C8 (R5C8=4, R5C8=7). Within the cell it excludes the other candidate; between cells it excludes the visible conflicting candidates R5C8=7, R6C8=4, R6C9=4.

Step 12 · Conclusion

The two endpoint cases exhaust all possibilities and both cross out R6C8=4, R6C9=4. Withdraw the temporary states, then remove them.
