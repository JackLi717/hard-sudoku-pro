Unique Rectangle Type 4 · Step-by-step example

Two corners hold only {2,7}; the other two share extras. A strong link for one pair digit across the extra corners forces the other pair digit out. Remove the opposite pair digit from the two extra corners.

Recognize the pattern

Two corners hold only {2,7}; the other two share extras. A strong link for one pair digit across the extra corners forces the other pair digit out.

How to find it

Find the two extra corners and check an exact two-place link on one pair digit in their row, column, or box.

What it allows

Remove the opposite pair digit from the two extra corners.

Avoid the mix-up

A shared line or box is not enough: the selected digit must have exactly two possible places in that region.

Step 1 · Where to look

This argument assumes the puzzle has exactly one solution. These four cells occupy two rows, two columns and two boxes. Swapping the two digits would preserve every region.

Step 2 · Find the strong link

The legend identifies the exact bivalue corners and the extra-candidate corners. We will call 4 the link digit and 9 the other rectangle digit. In row 1, the link digit has only the two extra-candidate corners as positions, so exactly one of them contains it. The other rectangle digit has additional positions in the region, so the two digits are not symmetric.

1: Exact bivalue corners (4, 9) · 2: Extra-candidate corners

Step 3 · Case 1: assume R1C2=9

Assume the other rectangle digit at R1C2=9. The strong link forces the other extra-candidate corner R1C5 to the link digit 4. The values shown then make the rectangle swappable; exchanging 4, 9 gives a second solution. Therefore the assumption is false.

1: Exact bivalue corners (4, 9) · 2: Extra-candidate corners

Step 4 · Case 2: assume R1C5=9

Assume the other rectangle digit at R1C5=9. The strong link forces the other extra-candidate corner R1C2 to the link digit 4. The values shown then make the rectangle swappable; exchanging 4, 9 gives a second solution. Therefore the assumption is false.

1: Exact bivalue corners (4, 9) · 2: Extra-candidate corners

Step 5 · Remove the other rectangle digit

The other rectangle digit at either extra-candidate corner creates the same swappable rectangle, so remove R1C2=9, R1C5=9. Keep the link digit 4: its strong link in row 1 must remain.

What it allows
