Hidden Rectangle · Step-by-step example

A 2·7 rectangle has two exact strong links that would otherwise force a deadly alternating pair. Remove that pair digit from the target corner.

Recognize the pattern

A 2·7 rectangle has two exact strong links that would otherwise force a deadly alternating pair.

How to find it

Trace both strong links through the four corners and identify the corner whose pair digit would close the deadly rectangle.

What it allows

Remove that pair digit from the target corner.

Avoid the mix-up

Check both links and all four corners. One link alone does not prove the elimination.

Step-by-step example · Top left

This example starts from a saved intermediate position. Its candidates include earlier logical eliminations; they are not fresh automatic notes.

Blue: evidence. Green: conclusion. Crossed-out digits: eliminated candidates. R1C1 means row 1, column 1.

Step 1 · Where to look

This argument assumes the puzzle has exactly one solution. These four cells occupy two rows, two columns and two boxes. Swapping the two digits would preserve every region.

Step 2 · Follow the two strong links

R6C8 is an exact bivalue corner containing only 2, 6. The target R5C1 is its diagonal corner because it lies in the rectangle’s other row and other column. Candidate 2 forms a row strong link through R5C1 in row 5 and a column strong link through it in column 1.

Step 3 · Assume R5C1=6

If R5C1=6, R5C1 cannot contain 2. The row strong link forces R5C8 to 2, and the column strong link forces R6C1 to 2; the exact bivalue corner R6C8 becomes 6. Exchanging 2, 6 in the resulting rectangle gives a second solution, so the assumption is false.

Step 4 · Remove the diagonal candidate

Remove R5C1=6. Candidate 6 at R5C1 would create the two-solution rectangle shown above.

What it allows

Remove R5C1=6. Candidate 6 at R5C1 would create the two-solution rectangle shown above.
