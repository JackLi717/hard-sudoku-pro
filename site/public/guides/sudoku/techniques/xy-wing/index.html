XY-Wing · Step-by-step example

A two-candidate pivot {X,Y} sees two wings {X,Z} and {Y,Z}. The wings may connect through a box, a row, or a column. Remove the shared third digit from a candidate that sees both wings.

Recognize the pattern

A two-candidate pivot {X,Y} sees two wings {X,Z} and {Y,Z}. The wings may connect through a box, a row, or a column.

How to find it

Find a two-candidate pivot, then two other pairs that each share one different pivot digit and the same third digit.

What it allows

Remove the shared third digit from a candidate that sees both wings.

Avoid the mix-up

The pivot itself must have exactly two candidates. The target must see both wings; seeing only the pivot is insufficient.

Step-by-step example · Row link

This example starts from a saved intermediate position. Its candidates include earlier logical eliminations; they are not fresh automatic notes.

Blue: evidence. Green: conclusion. Crossed-out digits: eliminated candidates. R1C1 means row 1, column 1.

Step 1 · Find the pivot and wings

R5C8 is the pivot with candidates 4, 7. It sees the two wings: R4C9 with 6, 7, and R5C2 with 4, 6.

Step 2 · Find the shared target

Both wings contain 6. Every target (R4C2=6) sees both wings, so it is their shared target for candidate 6.

Step 3 · Case 1: R5C8 is 4

If R5C8=4, R5C2 cannot be 4 and must be 6.

Step 4 · Case 1: remove target 6

Every target (R4C2=6) sees R5C2, which is 6 in this case. The target therefore cannot be 6.

Step 5 · Case 2: R5C8 is 7

If R5C8=7, R4C9 cannot be 7 and must be 6.

Step 6 · Case 2: remove target 6

Every target (R4C2=6) sees R4C9, which is 6 in this case. The target therefore cannot be 6.

Step 7 · Combine the two cases

The pivot can only be 4, 7. In either case one wing is 6, so remove R4C2=6.

What it allows

The pivot can only be 4, 7. In either case one wing is 6, so remove R4C2=6.
