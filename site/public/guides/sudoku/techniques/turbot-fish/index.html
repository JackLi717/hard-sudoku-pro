Turbot Fish · Step-by-step example

Two strong links for one digit connect through a weak link; the shape may resemble a skyscraper, a kite, or an empty rectangle. Remove the digit from any candidate that sees both outer endpoints.

Recognize the pattern

Two strong links for one digit connect through a weak link; the shape may resemble a skyscraper, a kite, or an empty rectangle.

How to find it

Trace two strong links and their weak connection. Identify the two outer endpoints before looking for a shared target.

What it allows

Remove the digit from any candidate that sees both outer endpoints.

Avoid the mix-up

Two highlighted pairs are not enough without a valid weak connection. A candidate seeing only one outer end cannot be removed.

Step-by-step example · Skyscraper

This example starts from a saved intermediate position. Its candidates include earlier logical eliminations; they are not fresh automatic notes.

Blue: evidence. Green: conclusion. Crossed-out digits: eliminated candidates. R1C1 means row 1, column 1.

Step 1 · See the shared four-candidate chain

Turbot Fish is the umbrella name for a four-candidate X-Chain. Skyscraper, Two-String Kite, and a two-candidate Empty Rectangle are named layouts of this same chain. Follow the four circles for 3.

Step 2 · Two places in row 3

In row 3, 3 can only go in R3C1 or R3C5. One of them must be 3.

Step 3 · Two places in row 8

In row 8, 3 can only go in R8C2 or R8C5. One of them must be 3.

Step 4 · Connect the two strong links

R3C5 and R8C5 see each other in column 5. They form the weak link: they cannot both be 3. This layout is Skyscraper; here we are viewing its shared Turbot Fish chain.

Step 5 · Try an assumption

What if R7C1 were 3? Numbers marked ? are part of this assumption, not confirmed answers. R3C1 shares column 1 with R7C1, so it cannot also be 3. R8C2 shares box 7 with R7C1, so it cannot also be 3.

Step 6 · Two identical digits in one region

R3C1 cannot be 3, so row 3 has only R3C5 left. Under this assumption, it must be 3. R8C2 cannot be 3, so row 8 forces R8C5 to be 3. But R3C5 and R8C5 share column 5: that would put 3 there twice!

Step 7 · The assumption cannot be right

For each target, assuming 3 repeats that digit in column 5. Remove candidate 3 from R7C1; all temporary assumptions are withdrawn.

What it allows

For each target, assuming 3 repeats that digit in column 5. Remove candidate 3 from R7C1; all temporary assumptions are withdrawn.
