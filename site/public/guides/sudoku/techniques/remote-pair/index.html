Remote Pair · Step-by-step example

Recognize the pattern

A connected group of identical two-candidate cells alternates the two digits across peer links. It can be a short or long line, or a longer branched network.

How to find it

Find matching two-candidate cells, trace their connected peer links, and assign alternating A/B states across one component.

What it allows

Remove either pair digit from a target that sees witnesses for both possible assignments.

Avoid the mix-up

All marked cells must have exactly the same two candidates. Check that the target sees the required opposite states rather than just two nearby cells.

Step-by-step example · Short line

This example starts from a saved intermediate position. Its candidates include earlier logical eliminations; they are not fresh automatic notes.

Blue: evidence. Green: conclusion. Crossed-out digits: eliminated candidates. R1C1 means row 1, column 1.

Step 1 · This is a Remote Pair

The marked cells form one Remote Pair coloring component using 1, 7. Round A and square B are its two opposite assignments. Keep the target R4C1=7 in view.

Step 2 · Confirm the matching bivalue cells

Every chain cell—R1C2, R4C9, R1C9, R3C1—has exactly the same two candidates: 1, 7.

Step 3 · Follow the alternating chain

Along the selected continuous path, connected cells see each other and must swap 1, 7. Round A and square B therefore alternate as opposite states within the same component.

Step 4 · Case 1: A is 1, B is 7

A gives R4C9=1; B gives R3C1=7. The target R4C1=7 sees both digits in this assignment.

Step 5 · Case 2: A is 7, B is 1

A gives R4C9=7; B gives R3C1=1. The target R4C1=7 sees both digits in this assignment.

Step 6 · Remove the remote pair from the target

Both possible assignments put one pair digit at each witness, so remove R4C1=7.

What it allows
