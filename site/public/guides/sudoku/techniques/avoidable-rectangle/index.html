Avoidable Rectangle · Step-by-step example

An avoidable rectangle combines placed digits and candidates in four cells across exactly two boxes. Completing the alternating pair would create an interchangeable rectangle. Remove the candidate that would complete the deadly rectangle.

Recognize the pattern

An avoidable rectangle combines placed digits and candidates in four cells across exactly two boxes. Completing the alternating pair would create an interchangeable rectangle.

How to find it

Find the placed and candidate corners, then test whether the target candidate would complete the alternating rectangle.

What it allows

Remove the candidate that would complete the deadly rectangle.

Avoid the mix-up

Unlike an ordinary unique rectangle, some corners are filled. Check the original givens and the exact two-box geometry.

Step-by-step example · Top left

This example starts from a saved intermediate position. Its candidates include earlier logical eliminations; they are not fresh automatic notes.

Blue: evidence. Green: conclusion. Crossed-out digits: eliminated candidates. R1C1 means row 1, column 1.

Step 1 · Where to look

This argument assumes the puzzle has exactly one solution. These four cells occupy two rows, two columns and two boxes. Swapping the two digits would preserve every region.

Step 2 · Identify the three player-entered values

The values R2C6=4, R9C4=4, R9C6=5 were entered by the player during solving; none is a given clue. The remaining corner is R2C4=5. Only player-entered values may participate in this swap.

Step 3 · Compare the two rectangle fillings

Completing the corner with R2C4=5 gives R2C6=4, R9C4=4, R9C6=5, R2C4=5. Swapping the rectangle digits gives R2C6=5, R9C4=5, R9C6=4, R2C4=4. Both fillings preserve every row, column, and box, so allowing the completion would create two solutions.

Step 4 · Do not complete the swappable rectangle

Placing R2C4=5 would complete a swappable rectangle with the three player-entered values. Remove R2C4=5; the player entries themselves remain unchanged.

What it allows

Placing R2C4=5 would complete a swappable rectangle with the three player-entered values. Remove R2C4=5; the player entries themselves remain unchanged.
