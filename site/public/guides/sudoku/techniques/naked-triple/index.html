Naked Triple · Step-by-step example

Three cells in one row, column, or box contain only three digits between them. Remove those three digits from the other cells in that region.

Recognize the pattern

Three cells in one row, column, or box contain only three digits between them.

How to find it

With candidates visible, compare groups of three cells in one region and count their distinct candidates.

What it allows

Remove those three digits from the other cells in that region.

Avoid the mix-up

The cells do not need identical candidate lists. The union must contain exactly three digits, with no fourth candidate in any selected cell.

Step-by-step example · Row

This example starts from a saved intermediate position. Its candidates include earlier logical eliminations; they are not fresh automatic notes.

Blue: evidence. Green: conclusion. Crossed-out digits: eliminated candidates. R1C1 means row 1, column 1.

Step 1 · Observe the triple

Together, these three cells have only 2, 3, 9 as candidates.

Step 2 · Understand the reservation

Digits cannot repeat in row 5, so these three digits must each occupy one cell.

Step 3 · Remove candidates

Remove the marked 2, 3 candidates from other cells in row 5.
